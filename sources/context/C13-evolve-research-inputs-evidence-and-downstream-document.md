## Process: 19 Steps

Execute every step. Do not skip steps. Document all findings in the Research Document as you go.

### Step 1: Product Understanding
- Document ALL features of the product
- Document ALL benefits (what each feature does for the customer)
- Identify unique mechanisms (what makes this product work differently)
- Map competitive positioning (how this product fits vs. alternatives)
- Record: features list, benefits list, mechanism description, positioning statement

### Step 2: Google Research
Run the following search queries (replace bracketed terms with actual product/problem/category):
- `"[product] reviews"`
- `"[product] complaints"`
- `"[product] vs"`
- `"best [category]"`
- `"[problem] solution"`
- Extract: complaint patterns, review sentiment, related search terms
- Record: top 10 findings per query, recurring themes

### Step 3: Amazon Research
- Find the product (or closest equivalent) on Amazon
- Mine the **top 20 positive reviews** (4-5 star)
- Mine the **top 20 negative reviews** (1-2 star)
- Extract exact VOC language -- the words real buyers use
- Apply Shulex patterns:
  - What do buyers love?
  - What do they hate?
  - What surprised them?
- Record: 20 positive quotes, 20 negative quotes, surprise themes

### Step 4: Reddit Research
Run these searches:
- `"[product] reddit"`
- `"[problem] reddit"`
- `"[niche] reddit"`
- Mine a minimum of 10 threads
- Extract: authentic language, pain points, desires, failed solutions people mention
- Record: thread URLs, key quotes, pain point themes, desire themes

### Step 5: YouTube Research
- Search niche terms on YouTube
- Analyze top 10 videos by views:
  - What topics get views?
  - What language do creators use?
  - What themes appear in comments?
- Record: video URLs, view counts, topic patterns, comment themes

### Step 6: Competitor Research
For each of the top 3 competitors (from Agent 01):
- Analyze their messaging and positioning
- Analyze their offers (pricing, bundles, guarantees)
- Tear down their landing page (headline, subhead, social proof, CTA, objection handling)
- Document their unique mechanism claims
- Record: competitor name, URL, pricing, guarantee, headline, mechanism, strengths, weaknesses

### Step 8: Creative Research
Categorize all ads found in Step 7 by:
- **Format:** UGC, static image, slideshow, VSL, talking head, demo
- **Hook type:** question, claim, story, demo, shock/curiosity
- **Messaging approach:** problem-focused, aspiration-focused, comparison, social proof, fear/urgency
- Record: categorization matrix with counts per category

### Step 9: TikTok Research
- Search niche terms on TikTok
- Find trending creators in the space
- Identify viral content formats being used
- Extract organic content insights (what gets views without paid promotion)
- Record: creator handles, viral video URLs, format patterns, hashtag themes

### Step 10: Instagram Research
- Scroll through Instagram feed/explore for niche-related ads
- Analyze ad format patterns being served (Reels, Stories, Feed)
- Note which formats appear most frequently (indicates what is working for advertisers)
- Record: format distribution, notable ad examples, creative patterns

### Step 11: Answer The Public + Answer Socrates
- Search the product name and problem on Answer The Public and Answer Socrates
- Document what questions people ask about the product/problem
- Group questions by: what, why, how, when, where, who, can, will, does
- Record: full question list grouped by type

### Step 12: Desire Mapping
- Compile ALL desires found across Steps 2-11
- Categorize into:
  - **Primary desires** -- the main thing they want
  - **Secondary desires** -- important but not the #1 driver
  - **Tertiary desires** -- nice-to-have, often unstated
- Record: complete desire list with source attribution

### Step 13: Objection Mapping
- Compile ALL objections found across Steps 2-11
- These are reasons people do NOT buy
- Include: price objections, skepticism, past failures, trust issues, logistics concerns
- Record: complete objection list with frequency counts

### Step 14: Failed Solutions Mapping
- Compile ALL failed solutions mentioned across Steps 2-11
- These are things people tried before that did not work
- Record: failed solution list with frequency, source quotes, and why they failed

### Step 15: VOC Phrase Extraction
- Extract the **top 50+ VOC phrases** from all research
- Rank by frequency (how often the same phrase or close variant appears)
- These are the exact words your market uses -- they go directly into ad copy
- Record: numbered list of 50+ phrases with frequency count and source

### Step 16: Competitor Gap Analysis
- Review all competitor messaging from Step 6
- Identify what competitors are NOT saying that customers clearly want (from Steps 12-14)
- These gaps are your opportunities
- Record: gap list with supporting evidence

### Step 17: New Mechanism Identification
- Based on all research, identify underexplored ways of solving the problem
- A "mechanism" is the HOW -- the specific way the product delivers results
- What mechanisms are competitors ignoring?
- Record: mechanism opportunities with rationale

### Step 18: New Information Identification
- Identify facts, studies, statistics, or data points that would be surprising to this market
- These become hooks and proof points in ads
- Look for: counter-intuitive findings, recent studies, expert quotes, little-known facts
- Record: information list with sources

### Step 19: Organize and Structure
- Clean all findings into the structured Research Document format (see Output section)
- Remove duplicates, resolve contradictions, ensure all sections are complete
- Verify G2 gate criteria are met before handoff

## Output: Research Document

The final Research Document must contain all of the following sections:

### Section A: VOC Language Database
- Minimum 50 phrases, frequency-ranked
- Each phrase includes: exact quote, frequency count, source (Amazon/Reddit/YouTube/etc.)
- Grouped by theme (pain, desire, objection, surprise)

### Section B: Competitor Matrix
- Top 5 competitors (3 from Agent 01 + 2 discovered during research)
- For each: name, URL, positioning statement, pricing, guarantee, creative angles used, identified gaps

### Section C: Desire Map
- All desires found, organized by Primary / Secondary / Tertiary
- Each desire scored on:
  - **Urgency** (1-10): How badly do they want this right now?
  - **Staying Power** (1-10): Will they still want this in 6 months?
  - **Scope** (1-10): How many people in the market share this desire?
- Ranked by total score (Urgency + Staying Power + Scope)

### Section D: Objection Map
- All objections with frequency counts
- Grouped by type: price, trust, logistics, skepticism, past failure
- Each objection paired with potential counter-messaging

### Section E: Failed Solutions List
- What the market has tried before
- Why each solution failed (in the market's own words)
- Opportunity notes: how your product addresses each failure

### Section F: Competitor Gap Analysis
- What competitors are NOT saying
- What customers want that no one is offering/messaging
- Ranked by opportunity size

### Section G: New Mechanism Opportunities
- Underexplored ways of solving the problem
- Each mechanism with: description, evidence it could work, differentiation potential

### Section H: New Information Opportunities
- Surprising facts, studies, data points for the market
- Each entry with: the information, the source, why it is surprising, how to use it in creative
