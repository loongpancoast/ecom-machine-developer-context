### 1.1 Four Entity Types (from Module 0)

| Entity Type | What They Do | Best For | Pros | Cons |
|-------------|-------------|----------|------|------|
| **Factory (Manufacturer)** | Makes the product directly | Brands at scale, custom products | Best pricing, direct control, IP protection | High MOQs, need QC expertise, language barriers |
| **Trade Company** | Middleman between you and factories | Beginners who need hand-holding | Multi-product sourcing, existing relationships | Markup on pricing, less transparency, may gate-keep factory access |
| **Fulfillment Agent (DS Agent)** | Sources + ships individual orders | Dropshippers, low-volume testing | No MOQ, fast turnaround, handles logistics | Lowest margins, no product control, no brand building |
| **Sourcing Company** | Professional procurement + QC + logistics | Scaling brands (1M+/yr) | Full transparency, credit terms, factory visits, QC oversight | Higher service fees, requires commitment |

### 3.2 MOQ Negotiation Tactics

| Tactic | How It Works | When to Use |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Start with their MOQ, negotiate down** | Ask what is the minimum for a first trial order -- often 50% of listed MOQ | First order with new factory |
| **Offer a deposit on larger future order** | Pay deposit on 5,000 but ship 1,000 first | When you need lower initial commitment |
| **Accept higher unit price for lower MOQ** | Trade margin for lower risk on first run | Testing a new product or factory |
| **Bundle multiple SKUs** | Combine MOQs across products to hit their minimums | Multi-product brands |
| **White-label first, custom later** | Use existing formulation/design, just add branding | Getting to market fast [REDACTED:PARTNER_SPECIFIC_EXAMPLE] |

### 3.4 Product Modifications for Differentiation

When moving from white-label to custom, prioritize these modifications:
1. **Custom packaging** (lowest cost, highest perceived value impact)
2. **Color/finish changes** (moderate cost, visible differentiation)
3. **Minor formulation tweaks** (for supplements/skincare -- add trending ingredients)
4. **Functional improvements** (based on competitor review complaints)
5. **Bundle creation** (combine existing products into unique kit)
6. **Custom tooling** (highest cost -- only when product is proven at scale)

## Original excerpt — S15:L189–L213

**Step 4: Sample Evaluation**
- Label every sample with factory name, date, and specs
- Test side-by-side across all supplier candidates
- Look for defects proactively -- play out real-world usage scenarios
- Ask the factory: What is the craziest defect you have seen and how did you solve it?
- A factory that can answer this honestly is a proactive partner

**Step 5: Certifications Check**
Certifications serve as a litmus test for factory maturity:
- Can they provide certifications immediately? (factory) vs. need to get them (trade company)
- Check for relevant certs: ISO 9001, GMPC, FDA registration, FCC, CE, UL
- Verify certification numbers independently -- do not just accept documents at face value

### 4.2 Red Flags Checklist

- [ ] Vague answers to technical questions
- [ ] Slow response times (>48 hours)
- [ ] Inflexibility on samples or small test orders
- [ ] Missing or expired certifications
- [ ] Will not provide factory visit or virtual tour
- [ ] Communication funneled only through sales (no engineering access)
- [ ] Cannot explain their QC process
- [ ] Unwilling to provide references from other clients
- [ ] Stock photos on their Alibaba profile
- [ ] Price too good to be true (may be cutting corners)
