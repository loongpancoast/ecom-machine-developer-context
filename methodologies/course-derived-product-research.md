# Product research: Ecom Masterclass, Evolve and God Tier Ecom

This is a source-derived operational summary of the historical internal adaptations and course notes, not a reproduction of paid course prompts or a claim that every transcript was reviewed. Ecom Masterclass is identified in the inspected catalog as EAM, taught by Zakaria (Airakaz). God Tier Ecom is GTE; the inspected workshop notes attribute it to Anthony Camacho. Evolve is a separate workflow lineage. Do not collapse them into one unnamed research prompt.

## Start here: research is not a list of trending products

Accept either a product/supplier URL or a customer problem. Save the market, exact variant, research question and source date. Produce a candidate decision, source-linked research document, brand/offer brief, creative references and a next-test hypothesis. Preserve rejected candidates and contradictory evidence.

Use the [field-by-field collection and cross-reference map](../history/copywriting-agent-systems/methodology-contracts.md) for what to scrape and retain. It covers ads, stores, reviews, Reddit, video comments, search, supplier quotations and first-party results. Every conclusion must resolve to the captured source rather than another generated summary.

## Ecom Masterclass (EAM): two different discovery routes

### Product-first discovery

1. Search permitted ad-library sources by customer desire or problem, not only generic discount terms. Use TikTok Shop and marketplace observations as additional candidate sources, not automatic purchase-intent or profit proof.
2. For each candidate record product/variant, discovery source, the desired outcome, evidence of urgency, audience awareness, competing solutions, possible differentiation and test/investigate/pass decision. Keep judgment labels separate from measured demand.
3. Follow the ad to the actual product/landing page. Capture offer, price, proof, guarantee, delivery promise, mechanism claim and objections addressed. Compare the exact product with supplier feasibility and landed economics.
4. Ask why someone would choose this product over an alternative. A viral clip, ad count or estimated store revenue is not the answer by itself.

### Problem-first discovery

1. Start in relevant public or authorized communities. Collect concrete situations, attempted remedies, reasons for failure, desired outcomes and naturally mentioned products.
2. Keep the exact useful language with its permalink and context. Do not turn a forum anecdote into a medical claim, testimonial or verified result for a different product.
3. Cross-reference mentioned solutions with ad libraries, competing offers and product facts. Include doing nothing, services, habits and other non-product alternatives.
4. Investigate underserved situations or objections, not merely an absence of ads. Verify whether a feasible product can actually serve the need.

### Research before the creative brief

The inspected EAM notes separate customer research, problem/root-cause research, solution-mechanism research and competitor/alternative analysis. The output is a brand road map: audience situation, desire, awareness hypothesis, competing claims, supported mechanism, offer, proof needs and creative/page direction. A marketing explanation must not be presented as established scientific causation.

EAM's awareness and market-sophistication lenses answer different questions: what the buyer already understands, and what competing promises they have already encountered. Neither is a measured fact unless supported by the research. Preserve the distinction between purchaser and end user.

## Evolve: broad research, then a structured downstream handoff

The inspected historical Agent 02 contains a 19-step sequence. The earlier [C13 excerpt](../sources/context/C13-evolve-research-inputs-evidence-and-downstream-document.md) omits its Step 7; a separately inspected historical Agent 02 explicitly includes Facebook Ad Library at that position. This is an additional source, not a silent repair of C13.

Operationally, the sequence covers:

- **Understand the product:** features, benefits, actual mechanism and competing alternatives.
- **Collect problem/customer evidence:** Google reviews/complaints/comparisons; positive and negative marketplace reviews; Reddit discussion; YouTube content and comments.
- **Inspect the commercial context:** competitor offers/pages and ad-library records; classify creative format, hook and message separately.
- **Broaden the reference pool:** TikTok and Instagram patterns plus question-research sources. Track organic and paid evidence separately.
- **Synthesize without losing citations:** desired outcomes, objections, failed alternatives, exact customer phrases, competitor messaging gaps, supported mechanisms and potentially useful new information.
- **Reconcile:** deduplicate, retain contradictions and assemble the research document rather than passing disconnected scrape summaries downstream.

The historical output sections are a customer-language bank, competitor matrix, desire map, objection map, failed-solutions list, competitor-gap analysis, mechanism opportunities and new-information opportunities. These feed audience/segment work, angle hypotheses, ad scripts, page copy and subsequent feedback. See [script handoffs](../sources/context/C10-evolve-script-structure-and-production-handoffs.md) and [feedback](../sources/context/C11-evolve-performance-to-next-batch-feedback.md).

**Historical corrections:** fixed collection counts are not proof of adequate research; ads running for weeks do not prove profit; traffic multiplied by assumed conversion and order value is a scenario, not revenue evidence. The new system must keep those estimates and assumptions visible.

## God Tier Ecom (GTE): language, desires, angles and belief research

The inspected Pain Diving notes describe a sequential evidence-to-copy process. Its useful operational distinctions are:

1. Start with a competitor/product and audience hypothesis, then retrieve and actually read relevant forum/community evidence. Verify generated URLs; never invent a corpus or infer individual demographics from a traffic estimate.
2. Supply licensed framework context separately from customer evidence. Loading framework text into a prompt is not model training or a fine-tune.
3. Build an evidence-backed audience situation and a set of desired outcomes. Do not invent a representative person's history or testimonial.
4. Draft distinct desire hypotheses before multiplying stories or angles. A **desire** is the outcome sought; an **angle** is a way to explain or frame it.
5. Connect each script or long-form sales-letter variant to its desire, evidence and landing-page promise. Historical numbers of variants and word counts are not current platform requirements.
6. Use measured tests to select a direction, then vary angles around that direction. A predicted winner stays labeled a hypothesis. Retain the control, changed variable, measurement window and decision.

The inspected copy workshop adds a practical research question: what existing belief or objection prevents this buyer from considering the product, and what truthful evidence could address it? Negative ad comments, failed alternatives and skeptical forum answers are inputs. Clarity and evidence matter more than a dramatic story. The notes attribute underlying belief/awareness concepts to Evaldo Albuquerque and Eugene Schwartz; no book or paid prompt text is redistributed here.

## Connect all three without blending their evidence

- EAM supplies product-first/problem-first discovery and the strategy step between research and copy.
- Evolve supplies broad collection coverage and structured research-to-agent handoffs.
- GTE supplies desire-versus-angle discipline and evidence-led objection/belief research.
- The current implementation adds supplier/sample checks, reconciled economics, source integrity and claims/rights review. No course heuristic establishes commercial success.

A usable brief contains: exact product facts and variant; evidence IDs; customer situations and language; desire/objection/failed-alternative maps; competing offers; supported differentiation; supplier/economics limitations; original angle and script hypotheses; proof and reference needs; page continuity; and a declared next experiment. The [production guide](before-after-ai-ugc.md) explains what happens next.
